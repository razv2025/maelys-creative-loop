Creative Loop · The problem2
The thesis

The compounding asset isn't the ad. It's the learning.

On Meta in 2026, everyone can generate infinite creative. The brands that win are the ones that learn fastest per dollar of spend — because targeting is algorithmic and creative is the last controllable lever. So the engine's job is not "make more ads." It is: make every dollar spent teach the system what to make next.

The load-bearing idea

The Creative Genome: measure attributes, not ads

Creative Loop · Data6
How performance shapes the next creative

Four concrete feedback mechanisms

1 · Learnings in the prompt

The brief generator is literally prompted with the current learning table: "creator-confession hooks +35% ROAS; explainer format −20%; quiz LP wins for problem-aware traffic." Every new concept must state which learning it exploits — or declare itself an exploration. No more starting from taste.

2 · Explore / exploit budget

70% of creative slots iterate proven winners, 20% test new hooks on proven angles, 10% are deliberate swings (new angle/format). The split is enforced by the concept queue itself — so the account never overfits to today's winner and never starves learning.

3 · Fatigue triggers refresh

CTR/thumbstop decay per angle (not just per ad) triggers refresh briefs automatically while the winner still profits: same proven angle, new hook and creator — launched before the decay reaches ROAS.

4 · Optimize to LTV, not first purchase

You sit on LTV data most brands don't have. Winners are re-ranked on 90-day LTV:CAC and refund rate per offer framing — critical with TBYB, where a "$0" frame can win CVR while quietly leaking into returns and chargebacks.

Creative Loop · Feedback mechanics7
Implementation prioritization

Learn from history first. Generate second. Automate last.

Sequenced by risk: each phase pays for itself before the next adds autonomy.

Crawl · weeks 1–6

Retroactive intelligence

  • Creative Genome schema + auto-tagger over the historical library
  • Join tags ↔ Meta / GA4 / LTV / A/B archive in the warehouse
  • First weekly Creative Intelligence report

Zero workflow change. Pure insight from money already spent — and the fastest way to earn the team's trust.

Walk · weeks 6–16

Learning-grounded generation

  • Brief & script generator with learnings in context
  • Ad→LP builder in production (Part 2 prototype hardened)
  • Veo teaser pretests for hook validation
  • Human approval gates on everything

Velocity ×3–5 at constant headcount; the team edits instead of drafting.

Run · week 16+

Closed-loop automation

  • Launch/test/retire rules via Meta API (buyer keeps override)
  • Explore/exploit allocation formalized (bandit-style)
  • LTV-objective optimization; quiz & LP personalization from ad context

The loop runs nightly. Fitting, for a brand whose product works while you sleep.

Creative Loop · First builds9
KPIs — and how each is actually measured

Measure the engine, the business, and the failure modes

Engine health
  • Creative hit rate — % of new creatives beating portfolio-median ROAS at the test-spend gate (e.g. $500)
  • Concept→live time — days, per creative, from queue timestamp to first impression
  • Learning velocity — validated learnings per month (hypothesis + confirming test)
  • Cost per winning creative — total test spend ÷ winners promoted
Business
  • Blended CAC & ROAS — Meta attribution cross-checked against GA4 + first-party purchase joins
  • 90-day LTV:CAC by creative cohort — the number that decides scaling, not day-1 ROAS
  • New-creative share of spend — dependence on aging winners is a leading risk indicator
  • Post-click: ATC rate & LP CVR per archetype — from GA4 events per generated page
Guardrails
  • Refund / chargeback rate per offer framing — TBYB "$0" framing must not buy CVR with returns
  • Fatigue index — thumbstop & CTR decay slope per angle
  • Compliance flag rate — unhedged claims caught pre-launch (target: 100% caught, 0 shipped)
  • Brand consistency score — sampled human QA of generated copy per week

Part 2 · Working prototype

I built the front half of the loop — it runs today

A Next.js app: drop in any Meta video ad (real MAËLYS creative included) and watch the loop execute — live Gemini video analysis, live page generation, and a real AI-generated next ad.

1 · Ad Intelligence

Gemini watches the full video natively — audio, on-screen text, pacing — and returns the creative DNA: hook + type, angle, promise, persona, awareness stage, claims, compliance flags, recommended LP archetype.

2 · Hook-matched landing page

Generates the page in MAËLYS's real archetypes, voice, offer mechanics, brand tokens and CDN imagery — extracted from a teardown of your five live LPs. Every section carries a provenance chip — ad analysis · maelys.com · internal data · competitor — so you can see each data source doing its job.

3 · Attribute-level performance

A (synthetic) fleet dashboard showing the Genome in action: ROAS measured per hook type, format, angle and LP archetype — "what the engine learned this cycle."

4 · The loop closes — with Veo

Learnings prompt three next-iteration concepts (safe / new-hook / swing), each with an explicit hypothesis — and one click generates a real 8-second AI video teaser with Google Veo.

What I found in your live funnel (20 Aug 2026)

Six findings from the GET-DREAMY teardown

Hook-match decays after the hero

The Caroline advertorial differs from the generic page by H1 + banner only — zero Caroline below the fold, though your own listicle already has the deep modules to transplant.

Speed leaks CVR before copy matters

VWO loads synchronously with 2s tolerance; 12 autoplay 1080p videos; zero lazy-loading on the flagship advertorial (the listicle has it — the fix exists in-house).

Three prices in one viewport

"$5 to try" badge, "| $0" button, "$43.20 $54 after" sub-line — dissonance at the exact decision moment, and a refund-rate risk hiding inside a CVR win.

Trust numbers disagree

1M vs 2M jars sold; "30 women/56 days" vs "33/28" on the same product; a hardcoded 15:06 countdown that resets every visit — visible to anyone comparing two LPs from two ads.

Friction at the ATC moment

The offer widget forces an unselected radio choice (the error state is pre-rendered); the two long advertorials have no sticky ATC — only the listicle does.

The quiz forgets its promise

It pledges a "$35 mystery gift," then the result screen never mentions it — and ignores the user's answers. Echoing zones/concerns + restating the gift is a free lift.
