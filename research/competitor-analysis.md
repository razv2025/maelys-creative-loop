## 1. Crépe Erase → The Body Firm (crepeerase.com → thebodyfirm.com)

- **URL fetched:** https://thebodyfirm.com/ (crepeerase.com 301-redirects here — **the brand has renamed from Crépe Erase to "The Body Firm"**, products still branded CRÉPE ERASE®). Fetched 2026-08-20.
- **Hero:** "END OF SUMMER EVENT / 25% OFF" — subhead: *"Transform crepey skin & restore a youthful look."* Secondary positioning claim: *"#1 Anti-Aging Body Treatment System"* with **NIQ (Nielsen) data attribution**.

### Offer structure
- Age-Renewing Body & Face Kit: ~~$79.95~~ **$59.95** (25% off); 2-Step Body Resurface & Repair Treatment: ~~$69.95~~ **$49.95**.
- Sells **multi-product systems/kits**, not single SKUs (Body Resurfacing Serum + Body Repair Treatment).
- **60-day money-back guarantee: "If you're not completely satisfied, simply return the bottles, even if empty."** (The famous empty-jar guarantee.)
- FREE SHIPPING sitewide; "FREE GIFTS (up to $178 VALUE)". Auto-delivery exists in checkout but is not foregrounded on the homepage.

### Social proof
- Clinical: *"93% experienced improvement in overall appearance of the arms and legs after 8 weeks"*, *"90% … look of elasticity after 8 weeks"*, *"83% … look of lifting sagging skin on the legs after 4 weeks."* Footnote: *"Based on a clinical study of 30 women using the Body Resurfacing Serum and Body Repair Treatment as a system"* — note the stat is attributed to the **system**, which sells the kit.
- Named MD endorsement: **Dr. Taz Bhatia MD** — *"This is the #1 brand I think of when my patients need help with their crepey skin."*
- Press: US Weekly, People, InStyle, Elle, Allure, New Beauty, Daily Mail. Before/after arms & legs photos. 3 named customer testimonials.

### Claim style
Heavily hedged, drilled compliance: "reduce the *appearance* of dry, crepey skin", "helps boost collagen & elastin", "helps restore elasticity", and **"Results may vary with Crépe Erase" repeated three times** on one page. Branded ingredient story: TruFirm®, "12 Super Hydrators", Squalane, AHA/BHA.

### Page structure (ordered)
Header → sale banner → hero (25% off) → "#1 Anti-Aging Body Treatment System" (NIQ) → two kit offers → transformation imagery → benefits checklist (paraben-free etc.) → favorites grid → clinical stats → "The Science of Crépe Erase" (ingredients) → Dr. Taz endorsement → press logos → testimonials → charity/cruelty-free commitment → promo banner → money-back guarantee → footer.

### vs MAËLYS
**They do, MAËLYS doesn't:**
1. **Empty-jar 60-day MBG** — post-purchase risk reversal you can use the whole product against. MAËLYS's TBYB is pre-purchase only; after the 21-day trial charge, there's no advertised guarantee.
2. **Third-party market-data category claim** — "#1 Anti-Aging Body Treatment System" per NIQ reads as measured fact, a rung above "Ulta #1 best-seller".
3. **System selling with a system-attributed clinical stat** — the footnote makes the 93% only claimable if you buy both steps; the proof itself is the bundle pitch. MAËLYS's 90/83/80 stats are single-product, so its RE-LURE upsell carries no proof of its own.

**MAËLYS does better:** (a) $5 TBYB trial is a far lower-friction front-end than pay-$60-then-request-refund; (b) MAËLYS's UGC density (125K+ videos, tagged before/afters with names+ages) feels 2020s-native vs The Body Firm's dated infomercial-era testimonial style.

## 2. Truly Beauty — The Firming Body Serum (trulybeauty.com)

- **URL fetched:** https://www.trulybeauty.com/products/the-firming-body-serum. Fetched 2026-08-20.
- **Hero:** "The Firming Body Serum — For Sagging Skin & Stretch Marks." Sub: *"An advanced peptide and bakuchiol serum designed to lift, tighten, and improve the appearance of stretch marks."*

### Offer structure
- $29 one-time; **Subscribe & Save 10%** ($26.10); **multi-pack ladder: 2-Pack $26.10/each, 3-Pack $24.65/each** ("Most Popular" badge on the 2-pack subscription).
- **Truly+ membership: "Save up to 20% with Truly+"** — a paid/loyalty tier above ordinary subscribe-and-save.
- Money-back-guarantee + free-shipping trust badges (terms unspecified on-page). Countdown timer at top tied to promo code "GLOSSY".
- Related: BodySculpt Firming Duo (bundle), Cellulite Serum with Caffeine + Forskolin ($39) — they run a whole firming sub-line.

### Social proof
Notably thin **on the PDP itself** in server-rendered HTML: no review counts, no clinical stats, no before/afters, no press (review widget may be JS-injected, but the static page a crawler/first-paint sees carries none). Truly's proof lives off-page in TikTok/Instagram creator volume. Elsewhere on site: "clinically proven to reduce stretch marks & cellulite in just 30 days" (BodySculpt Duo).

### Claim style
Hedged but with one aggressive outlier: "Visibly firms sagging skin", "Improves the look of stretch marks and cellulite", plus the striking consumption-framing claim **"See results from the 2nd bottle"** — which simultaneously manages expectations and justifies the 2-/3-pack. FDA disclaimer footer: *"These statements have not been evaluated by the FDA…"*

### Page structure (ordered)
Gallery → title/benefits/certifications → pricing + subscription/pack options → trust badges → "What Makes This Product So Good?" → key ingredients + INCI → "Save on Sets" → usage → "What it Targets" → subscription benefits → related carousel → footer.

### vs MAËLYS
**They do, MAËLYS doesn't:**
1. **Multi-pack per-unit price laddering** (2-pack/3-pack with falling unit price) as a non-subscription AOV lever. MAËLYS's only ladders are subscribe (20%) and the RE-LURE duo upsell.
2. **"See results from the 2nd bottle"** — expectation-setting that converts a weakness (slow visible results) into a multi-unit purchase reason. MAËLYS says "consistency is key" but never attaches it to a quantity.
3. **Named membership tier (Truly+, up to 20%)** — a loyalty identity beyond a checkbox discount.

**MAËLYS does better:** (a) on-page proof stack — clinical stats, footnotes, B/A walls, press, advisory board vs Truly's essentially proof-free PDP; (b) offer clarity — one hero product, one trial mechanic vs Truly's promo-code noise.

**MAËLYS does better:** (a) risk reversal — SDJ has zero trial mechanic ($48 up front; free returns is the only net); (b) problem-solution depth for the weight-loss/loose-skin persona — SDJ's page would not convert a skeptical "nothing works" GLP-1 customer the way MAËLYS's mechanism→clinical→objection arc does.

## Cross-competitor synthesis

**Where MAËLYS already leads the set:** front-end risk reversal ($5 TBYB trial — nobody else has one), ad-matched creator LP variants, advertorial narrative depth, GLP-1/weight-loss trend ownership, UGC volume, quiz micro-commitment funnel, countdown-integrated offer widget.

**Angle gaps — tactics running in this competitive set that MAËLYS's current LPs don't use at all:**

1. **Post-trial "empty-jar" money-back guarantee** (The Body Firm: 60 days, "return the bottles, even if empty"). MAËLYS's risk reversal ends when the 21-day trial charges; adding an empty-jar MBG after the charge would close the "what if it stops working / what if I miss the deadline" objection and is a proven DR device in this exact category.
2. **System-attributed clinical proof** (The Body Firm 93% "as a system"; StriVectin 94% "as a two-step process"). MAËLYS runs a GET-DREAMY+RE-LURE upsell with zero proof attached — a duo-specific stat (or even a re-framed existing stat) would make the upsell modal a proof moment instead of a plain discount.
3. **Multi-jar consumption laddering** (Truly: 2-/3-pack falling unit price + "See results from the 2nd bottle"). MAËLYS preaches "consistency is key" but never converts that into a 2-3 jar offer; a "90-day visible-results plan" pack is the natural post-trial keep-offer.
4. **Authenticity attestation on before/afters** ("Unretouched photos", StriVectin) and **pleasure/compliment-based secondary angle** (Sol de Janeiro's scent-first, "get compliments" framing) — both cheap copy-level additions; the second is a whole untested ad/LP angle for the same product ("the purple jar that makes your nighttime routine feel like self-care" vs. only problem-pain framing).
